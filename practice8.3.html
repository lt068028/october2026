🧩 Behind the Cues: Drill 3

Behind the Cues: Crafting Subject Questions with 〜が

Creating verb sentences with だれ (Who) and 何 (What) was straightforward, but doing so with どれ (Which one) and どこ (Where) was a genuine creative struggle.

1. だれ vs. 何: Human Freedom vs. Inanimate Action

だれ is used strictly for people—and human verbs are virtually limitless. People can teach, cook, help, or speak.

何 is non-human, so you cannot assign it just any verb. However, intransitive verbs (自動詞) naturally allow objects and animals to take active subject position (〜が). That's why we drew on early Genki intransitive verbs: ある (inanimate existence), いる (animate existence), and 泳ぐ (to swim: 泳いでいる with どの魚 and コイ).

2. The Puzzle of どれ: Inanimate Choice

While どれ inherently implies selecting one from a plural lineup, the real constraint lies deeper: どれ cannot be used for people, nor even for animals—it is restricted strictly to inanimate things. Furthermore, within Genki Lessons 1–8, the repertoire of intransitive verbs that can naturally pair with inanimate objects is very slim.

What inanimate things can naturally take action verbs? Vehicles can move (行く, 来る: どのバスが、来ますか), and events can unfold (はじまる, おわる: どの映画が、はじまっていますか). That gave us our solution for どれ.

3. How Can どこ Take a Verb? Organizations as Subjects

どこ is fundamentally a word of place. How on earth can a location take a verb as an active subject?

In natural Japanese, there is a cultural and linguistic convention of using どこ to refer to the organization or entity that occupies or owns a place. Because organizations consist of people, they can act as collective subjects! This allowed us to craft natural questions with つくる (フランスの会社が、作ります) and やすむ (田中さんのチームが、休みます).

This was only possible because we developed the two-step formula of pairing どの＋名詞 to make the referent concrete. Without narrowing down to どの会社 or どのチーム, a standalone question like "どこが休む？" would be far too context-dependent for learners to grasp as a universal pattern.

4. Ever Wonder Why There Is No Verb Drill for いつ?

Did you notice that いつ (When) has an Adjective drill (Drill 4), but no Verb drill?

In Japanese, いつ almost never functions as the grammatical subject of an action verb. Across all 23 lessons of the Genki series, the only plausible verb candidates found were intransitive state verbs like "いつが空いている？" (When is it open/available?) or "いつが混んでいる？" (When is it crowded?). Expressions where time itself acts as the subject (〜が) are extraordinarily rare.

5. どれ vs. どの＋Noun: When Is the Category Known?

Why do our drills always move from どれ (or だれ / 何) in the first line to どの＋名詞 in the second line?

  • どれ (Which one): Used when the category of options is unstated or completely open. You simply ask "Which one?" among visible items without naming what kind of things they are.
  • どの＋Noun (Which [Noun]): Used when the general noun category (e.g. 映画, バス, 会社, 店員) is already established and mutually understood between speakers. You narrow the focus from the known category down to the specific item.

This 2-step progression trains learners to smoothly transition from broad subject questions to targeted, category-anchored inquiries.

6. Semantic Symmetry: Adjectives & Question Words

While adjectives are more flexible than verbs, pairing them with question words reveals an elegant semantic symmetry. Because each question word targets a distinct ontological realm, it demands a specific category of adjectives:

Question Word Subject Realm Adjective Category Practice 8.3 Examples
いつ (When) Time & Schedule Availability & Timing ひま, 忙しい, いい
だれ (Who) Human Personality & Traits 親切, やさしい, 歌が上手
何 (What) Concept & Activity Experience & Preference おもしろい, 楽しい, 好き, 難しい
どれ (Which) Inanimate Object Physical Traits & Value 高い, 新しい, ない
どこ (Where) Space & Place Environment & Proximity 静か, ちかい, きれい

Notice that with いつ, the answer must inherently be a time expression (水曜日, クリスマス, 午後) because time can only take adjectives of schedule and convenience. You could never ask "いつが赤いですか". Each question word carries its own realm of descriptive reality!

Reflections on Question Words

Each question word possesses inherent semantic traits. Examining whether each one can legitimately serve as an active subject (〜が) illuminates the unique grammatical boundaries and real-world essence of Japanese question words with complete clarity.
